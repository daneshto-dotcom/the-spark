## S168 (2026-09-08) — five owner-reported bugs, seven boss skills across four bosses (PROTOCOL 45), and a three-agent hunt that found four regressions I shipped the same day

- P1 #the-reducer-was-innocent-and-that-is-why-it-survived — `raidParity.test.ts` had proved bot and human raids byte-identical for sessions, and it was RIGHT. Neither half of the owner's complaint lived in the reducer: one was arithmetic (a working tower is pinned to a 3-6 connector shape, so R76's "big lattices absorb raids" could never protect the thing it was written for), and one was the input picker (a 34 px creature radius swallowing an 8 px bond click). A green test that is TRUE can still be pointing away from the defect.

- P9 #tighten-the-guard-not-the-tolerance — my new letterbox rule flagged the Kraken's DIE row: three columns at the frame edge. The tempting fix was to raise the pixel tolerance. The right one was to require a MINIMUM BAR WIDTH, because a sliver is not a bar. Raising the tolerance would have hidden small REAL bars forever, which is the guard quietly ceasing to be one.

- P9 #fixing-the-reported-defect-un-masked-the-one-underneath — removing the scarab's black bars turned check:atlas RED on a size mismatch, because the bars had been inflating the bbox that `normaliseStateScale` measures. That WAS the owner's S165 "walk row too small" complaint, closed three sessions earlier without the cause ever being found. Two reports, three sessions apart, one root cause.

- P8 #look-at-the-frames-instead-of-re-rolling — the regenerated Kraken clip was STILL pillarboxed and the cheap move was another generation at his expense. A contact sheet showed veo had also ZOOMED despite a static-camera instruction, so no prompt was going to fix it and `sampleStart` did. He had just said "dont waste my money"; the way to honour that was to look at the output rather than re-ask the model.

- P7 #a-recommendation-can-be-correct-and-still-wrong — S167 proposed widening the deferred-death batch so one hook catches every kill. It does. It also hands every turret-killed creature a free posthumous swing, because deferral exists so a creature lands its committed strike. A death skill does not need a death HOOK; it needs to notice an ABSENCE, which is path-independent by construction.

- P7 #ask-who-else-touches-it-before-trusting-your-own-bracket — my first roster cut snapshotted at the top of the tick and compared at the bottom, and missed a player RAID, which is dispatched from main.ts's intent drain OUTSIDE runHostTick. - P2 #two-of-my-own-tests-failed-honestly-and-both-were-real — the tier-3 tower emitted exactly ONCE forever (a missing `sourceSpawnerId` routed the spawn into the Voltkin one-per-type gate) and my own harness advanced `w.tick` twice per iteration, so three windows looked like six units. - P6 #an-accusation-in-the-ui-needs-evidence-behind-it — the panel told the owner his TURN credentials were wrong; the verdict keyed on `relay === 0` with NO error code required, and the gather loop DISCARDED the one code that means "unreachable". ## S166 (2026-09-07) — the tier-3 race tower + its six units (PROTOCOL 43), the owner's spawner-portal bug (fixed twice), and the six tier-9 bosses + eighteen boss-structure states

- #distrust-agent-SIDE-EFFECTS-not-just-agent-claims — my standing rule was "verify what an agent CLAIMS". - #three-prompts-for-three-states-is-three-buildings — the owner rejected 18 tower images with one line: "the second state looks like a shack while the first one looks like a house". ## S165 (2026-09-07) — W1-C wired (PROTOCOL 42), the twelve zone backdrops, a 5-lane sweep (20 findings closed), seven owner playtest reports, and per-race music + two toggles

- #grep-the-archive-before-asking-the-owner — I put 'what happens to a dead seat's units?' to the owner as an open question. The real cause was a per-MODEL quota: veo-3.1 was exhausted while veo-3.1-fast and -lite accepted the same request in the same second. Check what still works before concluding what is broken.

- #two-samples-is-not-a-measurement — I compared veo-fast against full on TWO clips, found them indistinguishable at 200px, and said so. Across 24 clips the difference was real and specific: fast invents background scenery. Both statements were measured; the first was measured on too small a sample and stated with too much confidence.

- #fix-the-class-not-the-instance — cross-clip scale drift had been re-rolled by hand in S164 (the naga) and was about to be re-rolled by hand again. Prompting fixed 3 of 5. The durable fix was normaliseStateScale in the builder plus a guard — and the guard immediately found a SIXTH instance in already-shipped castle art that nobody had noticed.

- #a-guard-that-cries-wolf-is-worse-than-none — pointed at structures, my new guard produced two confident false positives (deliberate smoke read as scenery; rubble legitimately shorter than the intact building). Both checks silently assumed CHARACTER semantics. Scoping it explicitly, with those two false positives written up as the reason, is better than widening a threshold until the noise stops.

- #say-which-checks-actually-ran — the guard printed 'clean on both checks' even when --no-size had skipped one. That is the same false-assurance shape as the S161 gate that FAILED and was read as passing.

- #stop-chaining-gates-behind-&& — did it twice in one session after the project rule warns about it explicitly. Once it made a poll never run (and briefly convinced me two billed operations were lost); once it made python's exit code masquerade as tsc's.

- S165 P9 #read-the-number-the-error-printed — Three separate diagnoses of the same CI red were wrong before one was right, and the correct one needed no new evidence - it was in the first error string. 'waitForWorld timeout (15000ms)' names a budget; the wait it failed on declares 30_000. The two earlier theories (slow boot, then browser memory exhaustion) were both plausible reconstructions built ON TOP of the message instead of readings OF it. The memory theory even 'worked' - it changed the failure shape from a dying browser context to a clean single-test failure - which made it feel confirmed while the real defect was untouched. **RULE:** When a gate prints a NUMBER, find where that number is declared before theorising about causes. A timeout value that does not match the wait's own default is a bug in the plumbing, not a symptom of the environment.

- S165 P9 #an-asset-opinion-must-never-block-a-ship — A pixel guard written to protect art quality was wired into `npm run build`, so a missing pip package on the Pages runner took the LIVE DEPLOY down. Net effect: zero defects caught and a stale site while the owner was waiting to see new art. The same charter already said this about the bundle cap - 'never let it block a live deploy' - and I re-made the mistake one file over. **RULE:** Guards that express an OPINION about quality belong on a separate runner from the one that ships. Red CI plus a shipped artifact beats a blocked deploy every time: the owner then sees both the flaw and the warning, instead of neither.

- S165 P9 #a-partial-negative-control-accuses-a-working-test — I neutralised `idPhase` in drawChewBite, watched the jitter test keep passing, and concluded the test was vacuous. It was not - the function has TWO creatureId terms (ring PHASE and crumb DISTANCE) and my `grep | head -4` had truncated the second out of sight. With both neutralised it fails exactly as designed. Separately, a control I ran against the spawner-ignition guard silently matched NOTHING, because the goblin-tower line has no `if (...) return;` wrapper like its two siblings - so the 'test' I was validating was never exercised at all. **RULE:** A negative control must be ASSERTED to have applied, and must neutralise EVERY path to the behaviour. A control that cannot fail manufactures confidence; a partial one convicts working code. Print the mutated line, or assert the substitution happened.

- S165 P9 #never-assert-on-anything-a-wall-clock-touches — A prune test compared whole rendered frames between two renderer instances. It passed locally and failed on CI over `quadraticCurveTo(288,312,284,317)` vs `(...,286,317)` - two pixels of chewer leg - AND BLOCKED THE DEPLOY, because the unit suite gates it. The hop phase advances by `dtSec * IDLE_HOP_HZ`, i.e. real elapsed time, so the geometry encodes how fast the machine got between two syncs. Narrowing to the body position was machine-stable and stopped catching the leak, because a stale entry perturbs the HOP, not the position. **RULE:** Identify what quantity the claim is actually about before choosing what to measure. This one was a MAP SIZE; an inspection hook reporting it directly is immune to both the instability and the insensitivity. Two failed pixel-based attempts cost a blocked deploy.

- S165 P9 #a-lane-budget-is-a-shared-resource — Two new e2e specs took the shared gating lane from 4.1 to 5.3 min locally - past its 720 s Playwright cap on a 3-5x slower runner. That does NOT read as a failed test; it reads as `Timed out waiting 720s for the test suite to run`, i.e. the whole lane, which this project has already lost three weeks of signal to. My first fix trimmed the tick budget on arithmetic that looked airtight (cadence `(tick - seat) % 1800` implies seat 0 fires at tick 0) and was wrong: a probe showed zero race units at tick 1408, because the emitter needs `gameState === 'PLAYING'` and the transition lands a few ticks in, so every seat misses its opening slot. **RULE:** Adding a slow test to a lane at its ceiling is a change to every other test in it. Split it onto its own runner - the precedent already existed here twice - and prove the cadence with a probe before trusting the arithmetic about when a mechanic first fires.

- S165 P10 #scout-the-subsystem-before-designing-against-it — Two read-only scouting agents over the settings and audio subsystems, before any code, returned four defects that would otherwise have shipped: decoded PCM is 384 KB/s so an unbounded music cache reaches ~736 MB; NOTHING stopped the music at match end, which is invisible with one track and means 'you hear the previous race all next match' with six; the local race reads a confident, WRONG 'vampires' on the title screen for every player; and a third top-right glyph would have turned the deploy-gating e2e subset red via three separate guards. Each one is a defect I would have written and then debugged. **RULE:** For a feature that threads through an unfamiliar subsystem, spend the read pass first and demand file:line for every claim. The cost is one round trip; the alternative is discovering the same facts from a failing deploy.
